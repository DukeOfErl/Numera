## Four things to act on

**1. The aggregate is not the figure.** 64% is `basic` at 90% averaged with `compound` at 24%. Any
change read against the aggregate alone can improve it while making the simple questions — most of
what anyone asks — worse. This is why the tier exists (ADR-0530, task 4.13).

**2. Compound questions are where the harness is weak, and the weakness is search.** The
yeast-versus-*E. coli* comparison reached **0 of 24** expected entries on repeat 1 and 8 on the other
two. Two organisms in one question means the search has to cover both, and it does not reliably.

**3. Run-to-run variation is large enough to swamp a single-repeat comparison.** `yeast-nuclear-volume`
reached 2, then 9, then 9 across three identical repeats. Six of ten questions cited different
entries across repeats. **Do not compare two prompt versions on one repeat each**; this is the
question task 5.2 could not answer from the smoke run.

**4. Reaching the table is not being seen.** Expected entries land at ranks 17, 20, 22, 23, 29. The
CLI shows five rows. A reader would never have seen most of what "reached the table" counts, which
is a *ranking* failure in `entries_table._relevance` and needs a different fix from a search failure.

## An answer can claim an absence the corpus contradicts, and nothing catches it

`yeast-vs-ecoli-volume`, repeat 1:

> For *E. coli*, I don't have a directly comparable volume measurement in the corpus, but based on
> their smaller linear dimensions, yeast cells are vastly larger by volume—roughly by a factor in
> the range of 10–100 or more.

The corpus holds **eight** *E. coli* cell volume entries. That repeat made two searches, both
`property="size"`, and never searched volume — so the sentence is **true of its own retrieval
context and false of the corpus**. The estimated 10–100x does bracket the real ratio (~74, which
repeats 2 and 3 compute from the entries they found), so nothing here is fabricated.

**It passes every check we have.** `ungrounded_refusal` fires only when a whole answer refuses
without searching; this is a decline nested inside an otherwise complete answer. The retrieval
failure is counted — 0 of 24 expected entries reached the table, the worst result in the baseline —
but the *claim about the corpus* is not, and that claim is the part a reader acts on: told the data
does not exist, they stop looking. An incomplete answer invites a follow-up; a false absence
forecloses the question.

Task 4.14 carries it. Worth noting for hand-labelling (5.4): this answer is **sound** on
faithfulness — it reports its own context accurately and marks its estimate as an inference. Scoring
it down for the retrieval failure would count one defect twice, in the one file that is meant to be
an independent reference for measuring the judge.

## The property search is the underlying cause, and the mechanism is the opposite of the obvious one

Organism resolution is near-perfect: 87 of 91 cited entries carry the asked organism. Property is
54 of 91, and the agent searched the asked property in 20 of 24 questions.

`search` matches a property by **case-insensitive substring on the whole argument**, so a
multi-word property argument requires the stored value to contain that exact phrase in that word
order. Measured against this corpus:

| Search | Entries matched | Expected entries found |
|---|---:|---|
| `property="nucleus volume"`, yeast | 1 | 0 of 9 |
| `property="Nuclear volume"`, yeast | 4 | — |
| `property="volume"`, yeast | 63 | 9 of 9 |
| `property="cell volume"`, *E. coli* | 30 | 6 of 8 |
| `property="volume"`, *E. coli* | 86 | 8 of 8 |
| `property="cell length"`, *E. coli* | 2 | — |
| `property="length"`, *E. coli* | 47 | — |

So the agent searching `length` when asked about *cell length* is not the defect — it is the
**recall-preserving adaptation**, and narrowing to `cell length` would drop the pool from 47 to 2.
The cost is precision: 47 length entries include organelles and subcellular structures.

**The measured fix is to match on all terms present rather than a contiguous substring.** For
`"cell volume"` over *E. coli* that gives 48 entries and **8 of 8** expected — full recall at half
the noise of the single-word search. It does not solve `"nucleus volume"` against a stored
`"Nuclear volume"`, which needs stemming; that is a second step.

This belongs to `add-ask-agent` (`repository.py`), not to this change. Recorded here because it was
measured here and because it is the largest single lever on the figures above.

## Corrected 2026-09-16: the expected lists were still too narrow

The figures above are scored against the lists as they were on 11 September. Triaging the answers'
"cited, in neither list" entries by hand exposed a flaw in how those lists were built: they were
swept by **property wording** and truncated survey output, so entries recording a cell volume under
the word *size* were never seen.

The fix is to sweep by **unit dimension** — every entry for the organism whose units carry a volume
— then exclude by object and by kind. Wording plays no part. What that found:

| Question | Was | Now | Missed |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `ecoli-cell-volume` | 8 | 24 | five entries recording cell volume at five growth rates (109355–109359), and nine more |
| `yeast-cell-volume-conditions` | 17 | 20 | **100490**, *"Median cell size of a diploid budding yeast"*, 82 µm³ — the first entry the agent cites in every repeat |
| `yeast-vs-ecoli-volume` | 24 | 43 | the union of both |

The ribosome lists were confirmed complete by the same method, so the flaw was in the volume
questions alone.

**Re-scored against the corrected set** (`--expected-from-set`, which prints that it did so):

| Figure | 11 Sep | Corrected |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reached the table | 140 of 219 (64%) | **167 of 333 (50%)** |

**The headline correction is `basic`, not the aggregate.** "Basic questions are fine at 90%, compound
are the problem at 24%" was an artefact of a list built from wording. Basic questions reach two
thirds of what the corpus holds, not nine tenths, and the gap between the tiers is much smaller than
the first reading claimed. The direction of the finding survives; its size does not.

No re-run was needed: the expected list is never shown to the agent, so it cannot have changed a
single answer, and everything else required to re-score was already recorded.

## Four things to act on

**1. The aggregate is not the figure.** 64% is `basic` at 90% averaged with `compound` at 24%. Any
change read against the aggregate alone can improve it while making the simple questions — most of
what anyone asks — worse. This is why the tier exists (ADR-0530, task 4.13).

**2. Compound questions are where the harness is weak, and the weakness is search.** The
yeast-versus-*E. coli* comparison reached **0 of 24** expected entries on repeat 1 and 8 on the other
two. Two organisms in one question means the search has to cover both, and it does not reliably.

**3. Run-to-run variation is large enough to swamp a single-repeat comparison.** `yeast-nuclear-volume`
reached 2, then 9, then 9 across three identical repeats. Six of ten questions cited different
entries across repeats. **Do not compare two prompt versions on one repeat each**; this is the
question task 5.2 could not answer from the smoke run.

**4. Reaching the table is not being seen.** Expected entries land at ranks 17, 20, 22, 23, 29. The
CLI shows five rows. A reader would never have seen most of what "reached the table" counts, which
is a *ranking* failure in `entries_table._relevance` and needs a different fix from a search failure.

## An answer can claim an absence the corpus contradicts, and nothing catches it

`yeast-vs-ecoli-volume`, repeat 1:

> For *E. coli*, I don't have a directly comparable volume measurement in the corpus, but based on
> their smaller linear dimensions, yeast cells are vastly larger by volume—roughly by a factor in
> the range of 10–100 or more.

The corpus holds **eight** *E. coli* cell volume entries. That repeat made two searches, both
`property="size"`, and never searched volume — so the sentence is **true of its own retrieval
context and false of the corpus**. The estimated 10–100x does bracket the real ratio (~74, which
repeats 2 and 3 compute from the entries they found), so nothing here is fabricated.

**It passes every check we have.** `ungrounded_refusal` fires only when a whole answer refuses
without searching; this is a decline nested inside an otherwise complete answer. The retrieval
failure is counted — 0 of 24 expected entries reached the table, the worst result in the baseline —
but the *claim about the corpus* is not, and that claim is the part a reader acts on: told the data
does not exist, they stop looking. An incomplete answer invites a follow-up; a false absence
forecloses the question.

`add-judged-faithfulness` task 4.4 carries it, moved there on 2026-09-16: the defect is in what the prose asserts, and a phrase list would be brittle in both directions. **A faithfulness judge will not catch it either** — the sentence is faithful to its retrieval context, and the defect lives in the gap between what was retrieved and what exists, which that comparison cannot see. It needs its own criterion, given the expected entries as an input. Nothing catches it meanwhile, which is accepted rather than overlooked. Worth noting for hand-labelling (5.4): this answer is **sound** on
faithfulness — it reports its own context accurately and marks its estimate as an inference. Scoring
it down for the retrieval failure would count one defect twice, in the one file that is meant to be
an independent reference for measuring the judge.

## The property search is the underlying cause, and the mechanism is the opposite of the obvious one

Organism resolution is near-perfect: 87 of 91 cited entries carry the asked organism. Property is
54 of 91, and the agent searched the asked property in 20 of 24 questions.

`search` matches a property by **case-insensitive substring on the whole argument**, so a
multi-word property argument requires the stored value to contain that exact phrase in that word
order. Measured against this corpus:

| Search | Entries matched | Expected entries found |
|---|---:|---|
| `property="nucleus volume"`, yeast | 1 | 0 of 9 |
| `property="Nuclear volume"`, yeast | 4 | — |
| `property="volume"`, yeast | 63 | 9 of 9 |
| `property="cell volume"`, *E. coli* | 30 | 6 of 8 |
| `property="volume"`, *E. coli* | 86 | 8 of 8 |
| `property="cell length"`, *E. coli* | 2 | — |
| `property="length"`, *E. coli* | 47 | — |

So the agent searching `length` when asked about *cell length* is not the defect — it is the
**recall-preserving adaptation**, and narrowing to `cell length` would drop the pool from 47 to 2.
The cost is precision: 47 length entries include organelles and subcellular structures.

**The measured fix is to match on all terms present rather than a contiguous substring.** For
`"cell volume"` over *E. coli* that gives 48 entries and **8 of 8** expected — full recall at half
the noise of the single-word search. It does not solve `"nucleus volume"` against a stored
`"Nuclear volume"`, which needs stemming; that is a second step.

This belongs to `add-ask-agent` (`repository.py`), not to this change. Recorded here because it was
measured here and because it is the largest single lever on the figures above.

## Both directions of the three classifying rules, counted (task 4.10)

| Rule | Predicted | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| A stated quantity, from a citation **or any digit** | over-flags: a refusal mentioning a number gets marked | **never fired alone** — 24 both, 6 neither, 0 digit-only |
| The asked property was searched for | over-credits a broad argument | **20 of 23 credits** came from an argument that is not the asked property |
| A cited entry is an outlier among its peers | over-flags on a tight interval | **judged nothing**: 0 of 91 citations judged, 91 unjudgeable |

**The property rule's worst case.** `yeast-vs-ecoli-volume` repeat 1 asked *Cell volume*, searched
only `size`, twice, reached **0 of 24** expected entries — and is credited with having searched for
the asked property. It earns that credit on *one* shared stored property out of 492:

> Carbon:**cell volume** ratios determined from carbon measurements in bacterial cultures or in
> **size**-fractionated natural bacterial assemblages

One entry in 14,294 whose wording happens to contain both terms. The overlap distribution is sharply
bimodal — 0.8%, 2.2%, 2.2%, 14.8%×3, then 100%×17, with nothing in between — so any threshold from
20% to 99% behaves identically. Tightening costs six credits, of which two are really a fault in the
*set*: `asked_properties` of `Nuclear` and `diameter` are single words where the question asks a
compound property.

**The outlier rule is replaced, not tuned** — ADR-0540. Peers become the question's own expected
entries, restricted to the cited entry's organism and an identical unit string, which judges 56 of 91
and raises two flags that survive inspection, including 106356 at 10.3 µm³ against yeast nuclear
volumes of 0.28–3.3 — the entry that motivated the check and that the old rule could never reach.

A pool matched on unit **dimension** was measured and rejected: it put `1.8e-15 Liter` beside
`2.95 µm³`, both volumes, spanning fifteen orders of magnitude and destroying the lower bound. The
project has dimensional compatibility and no unit conversion, so sameness of unit must mean the same
string.

ADR-0260 recorded both as chosen rather than derived. They stay chosen: a bound that never fires
costs nothing, and raising a limit nothing has reached would be changing a number on no evidence.

The second half of the question — whether an answer produced *at* a bound scores worse — still cannot
be answered, because no answer was produced at one. What the distribution does show is that **effort
and success are not the same axis**: the two heaviest questions, at 13 and 11 tool calls, reached
8 of 24 and 15 of 17 expected entries. The most expensive answer in the set is among the least
complete. Whatever limits these answers, it is not the bound — it is the search.

Ten questions, three repeats, against corpus `a3364acbfa69fe90` (14,294 entries), prompt
`f8c6e7484a570bab`, code `git f934cfe`. **60 results, $0.45, one provider failure.** Results live in
`evaluations/`, which is gitignored (ADR-0170).

Run to ask whether a cheaper model can do what Opus 4.7 does. The corpus, the prompt and the
question set are the same as the 2026-09-17 three-model run, so the columns below are comparable;
only the code version differs (`f934cfe` against that run's `8d15303`).

## Ultra buys Opus's compound performance at Haiku's price

This is the finding. The 2026-09-17 run established that the choice of model is a decision about
`compound` questions — multi-organism comparisons and calculations — and that Opus's whole lead over
Haiku lives there: 62% against 31%.

**Ultra scores 58% on `compound`, for the same $0.40 that Haiku's 31% cost.** It gives up four points
of total reach against Opus (61% against 65%) and seven times the price.

It is worse than both Claude models on `basic` lookups — 63% against 68% and 70% — so the trade is
not free. But `basic` is the tier where all three were already close and where the 2026-09-17 run
found Haiku and Opus indistinguishable.

## Lightning is too weak, but it did not collapse

35% total reach and 18% on `compound` put it below Haiku everywhere, and six of its thirty results
ended at the retrieval bound rather than in an answer. It is not a MiniMax-style failure — MiniMax
reached 9% and ended 18 of 30 at the bound — but nothing here argues for it at any price.

Worth noting for what it costs: $0.05 for thirty questions, an eighth of Haiku.

## The unsupported questions need reading, not counting

The report's counter says Ultra declined **2 of 5**, against Opus's 6 of 6. Read, the picture is
different, and the counter is the thing that is wrong.

- **All three `planarian-cell-volume` repeats are clean refusals.** Each names the organism spellings
  and properties it searched and says it found nothing. They are flagged only because the
  stated-quantity rule counts a digit anywhere, and these refusals cite the size of the result sets
  they searched ("the 154 entries returned cover bacteria, yeast, human…"). The report says as much
  about its own rule; this run is a good demonstration of it.
- **`planarian-neoblasts` r1 is a clean refusal too.** r2 hit the retrieval bound and r3 is the
  provider failure, so neither is scoreable.
- **`ecoli-nucleus` is a judgement call, and Ultra answered it with a quantity all three times.** It
  said E. coli is a prokaryote with no membrane-bound nucleus and then gave the nucleoid volume from
  `[[100013]]` and `[[107009]]`, cited. That is not the Haiku failure of 2026-09-17, which invented a
  planarian cell volume from nothing. Whether a correctly-framed adjacent answer is the behaviour
  this question is testing for is a question about the question, not about the model.

Lightning declined 5 of 6, with one flagged refusal that reads as clean on the same grounds.

## What is not settled

- **This is one run.** The 2026-09-17 note's warning stands and applies here with more force: three
  repeats is the floor for reading any of this, and that run exists because a one-repeat comparison
  was read as a tie and was noise. Different entries are cited across repeats on 7 of 10 questions
  (Ultra) and 8 of 10 (Lightning).
- **Ultra's 13 entries cited in neither list are untriaged**, as Opus's 24 still are. Until they are,
  61% is a floor against a possibly-narrow expected list, not a ceiling.
- **One `provider_failure`** (`planarian-neoblasts` r3, 2 model calls, no error text recorded). One
  in thirty, transient, unexplained. Not enough to characterise, and worth watching if Ultra is used
  again.
- **The endpoint is not the model.** OpenRouter serves paid Ultra from DeepInfra (fp4), BaseTen (fp4)
  and Venice (fp8), and paid Lightning from Phala, CoreWeave and DeepInfra (bf16). Quantization
  differs between them and from NVIDIA's own free endpoint (nvfp4). These figures measure the paid
  endpoints as routed on 2026-09-21, not the weights in the abstract.
- **Nothing was changed.** `DEFAULT_MODEL` is still `anthropic/claude-opus-4.7`. Ultra at a seventh
  of the price is a real option, but one run is not the evidence to move a default on.

## Why not the `:free` variants

They were the original request. The free variant of each model has exactly one endpoint — NVIDIA's
own — whose data policy is `training: true, retainsPrompts: true`. Every question sends corpus
content to the provider: `search_entries` returns value rows and `get_entry` returns the entry's
prose, including `comments`, at a measured mean of 32.5 entries per question. Publishing that for
training is a broader redistribution of unlicensed third-party data than the one ADR-0170 and
ADR-0200 already refuse.

The paid endpoints are the same weights rented from hosts that do not train, and most of which retain
nothing — a better retention posture than the Anthropic endpoints the baseline ran on (30 days). They
cost $0.45 for both runs. The free variants were not worth the question they raised.
